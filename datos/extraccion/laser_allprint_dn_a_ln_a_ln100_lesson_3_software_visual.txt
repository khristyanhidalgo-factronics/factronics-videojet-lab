Template
The template defines the layout of the marking, e.g. texts, logos, serial numbers, fonts, etc.
With a double - click onto a template the template is opened in user interface »Template«.
Font
The database can contain several fonts. The required font is selected in the template.
You can use fonts of the format *.fnt or true type fonts (TTF).
With a double - click onto a font a preview of the font is displayed.
Logo
A logo is a graphical element added to the template. To be able to add a logo to a template the
logo has to be imported into the database.
At the moment the following formats can be imported:
plt, mcl, bmp, ai, lgo, gif, jpg, jpeg
With a double - click onto a logo a preview of the logo is displayed.
Parameter Set
Parameter sets adjust the laser system to the respective material of the product. A parameter set
contains e.g. information on the laser power and the marking speed.
With a double - click onto a parameter set the dialog with all settings of the parameter set is
opened.
Product Registration
The product registration contains all data necessary to detect the product and to trigger the
marking process.
With a double - click onto a product registration the software changes to user interface
»Configuration«, page »Product registration«.

Lets add another term VECTOR . A vector is a segment within a stroke or a curve. A curves line
may contain more than one vector. This depends entirely on how the fontographer (one who
designed the font) created the characters. The more vectors a character has the longer it will take
to write that character. This is due to the start and stops of the galvo mirrors. It is important to
select a font designed for high speed marking in most applications.
By now everybody must be familiar in how to get into the template and get to show the jumps
and strokes of a particular template. When the laser marks a code it tries to optimize the marking
time by adjusting the sequence in which the characters are created. To further emphasize this
below is a picture to show you the vectors, strokes and jumps that the laser will process in order
to make a mark. Note difference in direction of jumps and order in which characters are written
depending on the product registration set used on the template. This will help out in determining
which parameter will need to be adjusted.

This Jump Speed has to be balanced with the Jump Delay. The faster you do a jump speed the
longer the jump delay. Jump Speed should be kept to a minimum to avoid having to add more
Jump Delay to account for galvo vibration. These vibrations are normally seen on the first
character of a code or a vector after a long jump, because the mirrors have increased inertia.
In the message above, we have set the Jump Speed to a value of 40,000. The characters are
unstable at the major galvo jumps (beginning of new line). Setting a high Jump Value only
saved 3ms of code time. The code quality suffers at these high values. We recommend a start
value of 10,000.
Videojet Technologies Inc. 3 - 30 October , 20 14

Marking speed has to be balanced with Marking Intensity . Fuzzy edges of the lines or
incomplete removal of material are indications that the mark speed is to high based on the
current level of intensity. Lasing to deep or burning the substrate may not be the desired effect,
this could create puncture in the material when using a cross over font where lines intersect (If
burning is occurring where vector intersect it is recommended that a non - cross over font be use).
The faster your Marking Speed the lighter your mark will be so the Marking Intensity must be
increased accordingly. High Marking Speeds will also require delays to be increased. When the
galvos are moving at high speeds, they require more time to settle into position so that incorrect
character formation can be minimized.
When marking on hard substrate (i.e. glass) it will be necessary to lower your marking speed to
achieve the desired effect. Lowering the marking speed will could give you incomplete marking
fields. This is because the laser is taking to much time to mark. Raising the jump speed and
lowering the delays with help to composite for the lost time due to low marking speeds.
Videojet Technologies Inc. 3 - 31 October , 20 14

In laser marking the laser power class should match the application. The laser power must
be high enough to drive the material effects for the marking process and to fulfill process
speed demands. But the popular opinion “As higher the laser power available as better ...”
is an error that has already caused headaches to lots of users. The reason: a high power
laser trimmed down to a low - power level (for avoidance of unwanted material effects/
optimization of marking quality)
• I involves the risk of unstable operation
• Can cause quality and especially reproducibility problems
• Operates highly inefficient (wall - plug efficiency)
• Wastes capital.
The application department of an experienced laser marker supplier determines the appropriate
laser power class by test marking the customer samples under simulation of his production
conditions. And the customer is well adviced to obey these results .

Overview laser Parameters

Laser Operation Marking Adjustment
Template List
The templates stored in the laser system can be selected via the digital inputs DATA 0 to
DATA 7 (refer also to the interface assignment described in chapter "Appendix" of the laser
manual). This procedure is called External Template Selection .
Videojet Technologies Inc. 3 - 47 October , 20 14

A template is assigned to a certain number in the template list (see below). The number of the
desired template is sent - e.g. by a PLC - binary coded to the digital inputs DATA 0 to DATA 7
and is taken over with the signal EXT_EVENT. The selected template becomes current template
of the laser system and can be marked on the product.
Note for YAG systems
In order to use the external template selection the laser system must be equipped with two
optional input modules (art. no. AL - 65856).
Template Sequence
A trigger pulse starts a succession of templates which are marked on the product one after the
other. Trigger pulses coming in while executing a sequence are put into a trigger queue and
executed after finishing the sequence.
Template Batch
The templates of a batch are executed by consecutive trigger pulses. The
first pulse comes from the start trigger, all following pulses are consecutive
trigger pulses (product registration). If the execution is interrupted, it has to
be started again with the start trigger, i.e. the execution begins with the first
template in the batch.

Product Registration
»Start trigger« defines the settings for the first trigger pulse. The marking process is started by
the first trigger pulse.
With the trigger set to »Auto«, the marking process is started by pressing START on the control
panel of the laser system.
To start the marking process via an external signal you can define whether the rising or the
falling edge is to be used as trigger.
»Consecutive trigger« defines the settings for all trigger pulses following the start trigger pulse.
If the consecutive trigger is set to »None«, only the start trigger pulse starts a marking process.
All following trigger pulses are not accepted. Select »None« whenever you want to generate a
single marking.
With the consecutive trigger set to »Auto«, the marking process is triggered internally and
continuously.
The marking process is repeated with a certain frequency or after the product has covered a
certain distance - depending on the settings for the product movement.
To trigger the marking process via an external signal you can define whether the rising or the
falling edge is to be used as trigger.
»Stop trigger« defines the settings for a trigger pulse that stops the marking process (e.g. the
end of a cable or a stop signal from an external control unit). The laser system remains ready to
mark after a stop trigger pulse, which means that the marking process will be continued if
another start trigger pulse is detected.
If the trigger is set to »None«, only the start trigger pulse starts a marking process. All following
trigger pulses are not accepted.
To stop the marking process via an external signal you can define whether the rising or the
falling edge is to be used as trigger.
»Start delay« defines the distance until the marking process is started, which is the distance
between the center of the marking field of the laser system and the center of the marking on the
Videojet Technologies Inc. 3 - 52 October , 20 14

product. After the trigger the product has to cover this distance before is in front of the marking
head. If »No product movement« has been selected the start delay defines the time between start
trigger pulse and marking in ms.
»Blocking« defines the distance the product has to cover before the next marking can be started.
If »No product movement« has been selected the blocking defines the time that has to pass after
a start trigger until a consecutive trigger is accepted.
»Start blocking« sets the blocking time after the stop trigger pulse, where no external trigger
pulses are accepted (debouncing).
»Distance/Rot.« (1) defines the distance the product covers with one full turn of the incremental
encoder.
»Pulses/Rot.« (2) states the number of pulses the incremental encoder emits when performing
one full turn.
»Rotational direction monitoring« (3) defines the direction of rotation of the incremental
encoder.
It is used e.g. to cut out signals that derive from slipping of the conveyor belt. Select »left« for a
clockwise direction of rotation of the incremental encoder, or »right« for a counter - clockwise
rotation. Select »Off« to turn the monitoring off.
The definition of angles and the settings for the trigger correspond to 2. Registration: Fixed.
Important The number of pulses per rotation depends on the type of incremental encoder used
and on whether one or two tracks are utilized.
In case that two tracks are used, the number of pulses per rotation given for the incremental
encoder has to be doubled and entered at »Pulses/Rot.«.
Point out that the start distance can not be set at 0

Marking head
Marking head
Here the marking head installed in the laser system is displayed.
Select objective
In this list field you can select the lens type. The selected lens type has to correspond to the lens
installed at the marking head of the laser system. The focal length of the selected lens type is
displayed.
Working distance
The working distance is calculated by the system. Enter the values for a possible tube length and
deviations and the actual working distance is displayed.
Marking head
Here you can enter a translation, scaling factor or rotation that applies globally to all templates.
Zero position »X - Delta« and »Y - Delta« define the translation of the marking field. The
translation refers to the center of the marking field and is stated in millimeters.
Scaling factor »X - Scale« and »Y - Scale« are stated as a multiplier for the respective coordinates.
The default value is 1. The coordinates of x and y axes can be scaled individually.
Pilot laser
The system is equipped with an optional pilot laser you can enter here translation and scaling
values for the pilot laser independently from the settings of the marking laser.
Marking field
Here you can adapt the marking field, e.g. to translate the marking into the optimum area of the
lens or to adjust the marking position to a beam protection assembly.
If the option »Max. marking field« is active no other settings are necessary. If you want to define
the marking field yourself, deactivate »Max. marking field« and enter the desired positions of
the marking field borders.

TT Otechnology requires physical contact with target materials at high throughput and placement within other
equipment to reach that target film at the correct time. Planning and proper installation are critical to the success
of TT O printers. Post installation is less forgiving than in other technologies, so it is worth taking the time to get
things correct the first time.
Shock Hazard W arning
Lethal voltages are present within this equipment when it is connected to the mains electrical supply .
Unless it is necessary to run the printer , disconnect the printer from the mains electrical supply before
removing the covers, or attempting any service or repair activity . Failure to follow this warning can
cause death or personal injury .
Personal Injury
Improper use of this equipment may result in personal injury .
Possible Equipment Damage
Improper use of this equipment may result in equipment damage.
Possible Equipment Damage
Power the printer OFF before connecting or disconnecting any cables or equipment to or from the
printer . DO NOT connect cables or other equipment to the printer while it is powered on.

Customer applications that require TT Oprinters will vary and every installation is unique. It will help to familiarize
yourself with the most common machines in which our printers will be installed.
V e r t i c a l B a g g i n g M a c h i n e s
V ertical Bagging Machines operate in conjunction with multi-head weighers.
Continuous and intermittent mode available.
Usually used for individual bags, sachets, or packets.
The printer is generally mounted underneath the web path.
Figure 1: Illustration of a V ertical Form Fill machine.
V ertical Form, Fill and Seal (VFFS) applications use gravity to feed (drop) product into a partially formed bag
which is then sealed after filling. VFFS machines often have built in registration. The purpose of the registration
unit is to allow the print position on the packaging film to be moved into the required area.
Figure 1 shows the typical flow of the film packaging material in a VFFS application.
T ypically , the film is fed from a roll at the back of the packaging unit, up onto the top of the unit, and
onto the forming collar as shown in Figure 1 .
The bag is sealed on one side to form a tube (fin seal).
At the sealing jaws, the jaws close to seal the top of one bag and the bottom of the next.
When the bottom of the bag is sealed the product falls in and the jaws close to seal the top of that bag
and the bottom of the next, and so on….
VFFS machines (aka: baggers ) are limited in speed based on how fast products can fall.
The forming collar converts the film from a flat shape to a tube.
Print Quality Issues at the Collar
A print quality issue to watch for - The angle the film wraps around the collar is very sharp and
can scrape some of the image off of the printed film.
The sealing jaws seal the top of the filled bag and the bottom of the next empty bag that is being
formed.
Print Quality Issues at the Sealing Jaws
Print quality issues – what to look for - They are often heated so it is important to make sure
the printed image is not in this area of the package.

When assembling the bracket system, it is important to ensure the bracket is square.
Possible Equipment Damage
Do not over tighten bolts as this will result in equipment damage.
1. Place support tube blanking caps into the side plate support tubes and use a rubber mallet or soft
hammer to tap the support tube blanking cap until fully seated.
Figure 12: Support T ube Blanking Caps.
2. Repeat 8 times so that all caps are installed.
3. Assemble the printer mounting assembly .
4. Place (2) side plate support tubes into the printer mounting assembly .
Figure 13: Side Support T ubes latched to the Printer Mounting Assembly .
5. Fit the side plate support tubes into a side plate. These side plate support tubes are mounted opposite
the registration roller holes.
Ensure the recessed bolt holes are facing down.
If you will use a platen roller , install it at this time.
Figure 14: Side plate support tubes fitted into Side Plate.
6. Place the other (2) side plate support tube into the side plate.
7. T ighten the bolts that secure the side plate tubes to the side plate.
8. Place the second side plate on top. ensuring that your bracket is square.

Ensure both registration holes are on the same side.
Ensure the recessed bolt holes are facing up.
9. T ighten the bolts that secure the side plate tubes to the side plates.
10. If intermittent, assemble the platen pad and attach to the side plate support tube.

The platen roller is installed on continuous applications or in intermittent applications when the printed area is
greater than 75mm.
Figure 16: Platen Roller Layout
T o install the platen roller into the universal bracket. It is best to have only one side plate assembled.
1. Locate the holes for the platen roller .
Figure 17: Platen Roller hole in the universal bracket.
2. Fit the rod through the platen roller .
3. Slide one bearing to each side of the rod.

Figure 18: Platen Roller bearings.
4. Center the roller on the rod.
5. T ighten the bearing screws.
6. Attach the platen roller assembly to the side plates.
Figure 19: The Platen Roller fitted to the Universal Bracket.
7. T ighten the side plate screws to clamp the rod.
8. Repeat steps 5 and 6 on the other side plate.
M o u n t i n g R o l l er s
The bracket system uses many dif ferent types of rollers standard and food grade are the most popular types.
There are pressed and heavy duty as well.
Now that the bracket is built we can start mounting the diverting rollers. It is important that the diverting rollers
have been cut to the correct size.
Figure 20: Diverting Roller Layout
Food grade diverting rollers are dif ferent from the conventional diverting rollers and require a mounting tool that
comes in the bracket kit.

Figure 21: Diverting Roller (left) and Mounting T ool (right).
1. Place the bearing end cover cap over the side plate support tubes. If using food grade rollers follow
instructions for food grade rollers.
2. Insert the stub axle collar onto the stub axle.
3. T ighten the stub axle collar so that it is not slipping but not fully tightened. This will be done later .
4. Repeat steps 1-3 at the other end.
5. Place the diverting roller into the bracket at the desired bolt hole.
6. Using a wrench to lock the stub axle, screw in your bolt and tighten.
Figure 22: T ightening the Stub Axle
7. Repeat steps 5 and 6 on the other side.
8. Center your diverting roller .
9. Fully tighten your (2) stub axle collars.

T o assemble the registration roller use the following steps:
1. Attach the T oothed Rails to the side plates using M5 x 12mm screws. The counter sunk holes should
be on opposite side of rails.
Figure 23: End Plates with T oothed Rails
2. Cut the registration shaft. End-to-end dimension of the shaft including threads should be 2.5” longer
than the stainless steel support tubes on the universal bracket.
3. Thread the 10mm half nut onto the shaft. If nut is not available, skip this step and move onto step 4.
4. Thread the threaded pinion (without the set screws) onto the shaft.
5. Be sure the half nut and pinion is threaded all the way down to where the threads meet the solid end of
the rod. Insert the threaded end of the shaft through the end plate slot with the toothed rail on the
inside.
6. Insert a 10mm washer on the threaded side of the shaft, moving washer toward the end plate.
7. Thread the hand-wheel knob (one with threads all the way through the handle) onto the shaft.
8. Thread the 10mm full nut onto the shaft.
9. Thread second hand-wheel knob to end of shaft.

Figure 24: Completed Assembly of Registration Roller and Hand Wheel Slide
10. Cut the aluminum rollers approximately 2.75” shorter than the stainless steel support tubes on the
universal bracket.
1 1. Insert the bearing end covers on each end of the aluminum rollers.
12. Insert one aluminum roller onto non-threaded end of shaft towards the10mm half nut. If the half nut is
not available, insert a 10mm washer before inserting aluminum roller .
13. Insert shaft collar (small collar) onto non-threaded end of shaft. DO NOT SECURE THE COLLAR.
14. Insert the un-threaded pinion onto non-threaded end of shaft. DO NOT SECURE THE COLLAR
SETSCREWS.
15. Insert the other 10mm washer onto non-threaded end of shaft.
16. Insert second end plate with tooth rail on the inside.
Figure 25: Completed Assembly of Registration Roller Shaft on Non-Hand Wheel Side.
17. Insert stub axles into the end caps of second aluminum roller . Attach stub axle collar to each stub axle.
Attach second aluminum roller to end plates using M6 x 16mm screws.
Figure 26: Completed Registration Roller
18. Secure the Registration Assembly to the universal bracket using the connecting blocks and M6 x 20mm
screws.
R e g i s t r a t i o n R o l l e r A d j u s t m e n t s
The Registration roller can be adjusted so that the print window on the film is under the printer in intermittent
printing mode.
1. Push the registration roller all the way to one end. Make sure roller is straight.

2. Secure the shaft collar and pinion setscrews. The registration roller should travel freely and straight
along the rails without binding.
3. T o lock the registration roller in place, tighten hand-wheel closest to the end plate and tighten the full
10mm nut against the hand-wheel. Make sure the knob is threaded to a stop before securing the nut.

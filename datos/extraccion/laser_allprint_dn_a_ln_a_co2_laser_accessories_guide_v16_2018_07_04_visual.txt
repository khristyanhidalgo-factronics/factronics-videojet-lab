Safety First Eye Protection
Only engineers specifically trained on lasers, including laser safety can work with unguarded
lasers. When working with Class 4 CO unguarded lasers an engineer can use CE marked
2
polycarb safety glasses.
Engineers working with CO and solid state lasers need appropriate safety eye wear.
2
Safety goggles regardless of protection level are not intended for looking directly into a laser
beams. Laser safety goggles do vary in quality and protection level. Please see the
explanation of the specification that you will see on suitable laser eye protection, should you
decide to source locally the higher the R or L scale number the better.

Do I need a laser with 9.3µm
Sample Lab report will show you the most suitable wavelength
9.3 µm 10.6 µm
Recast
PET substrates generally show a different material effect when coded with either
10.6µm or 9.3µm. 10.6µm will generate a melting and engraving of the surface,
while 9.3µm will generate a foaming of the surface, which leads to a higher
contrast and is preferred by most customers for PET.

Filter Disposal
The relevant European directive is 91/689/EC Hazardous Waste.
This directive encompasses the European Waste Catalogue under which new clean filters would be classified
under item 15 02 03 absorbents, filter materials, wiping clothes and protective clothing other than those mentioned
in 15 02 02 This is a non hazardous category. However depending on the materials being lasered the fume which
is collected in the filters may be considered dangerous in which case the classification changes
to 15 02 02 M. This is potentially a Hazardous category depending on the amount of hazardous material in the filter
and the risk it poses. For the pre filter the risk involved would normally be R37 Irritant to respiratory system
caused by the very fine particulate trapped in the filter. However this only becomes hazardous if more than
20 of the waste (filter plus particulate) is made up of the hazardous material which is unlikely.
PVC applications have an added dimension in that HCL is generated and in some circumstances this could potentially
form an acid inside the filter which would be difficult to quantify so we recommend that the filter is treated
as a hazardous waste with a potential R34 Corrosive risk. The combined filter needs to be considered in the same
way.
The risks being different for different materials. Most applications will be non hazardous
because the of any hazardous material is likely to be below the threshold level. Again for PVC and other
polymers which generate highly toxic gasses it is safest to treat the filters as hazardous waste with a toxic risk.

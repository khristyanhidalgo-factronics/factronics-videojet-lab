General Safety
Class 1:
Not capable of emitting accessible laser radiation in access of
what the AEL allows for class one.
Example: Laser Printer, CD player
Class 2:
Not capable of emitting accessible laser radiation greater than
AEL allows with .25 exposure duration.
Example: Pilot laser and He - Ne alignment Laser
Class 3:
Potentially hazardous if focused at the eye or skin for less time
than what’s allowed for class 2
May cause acute eye hazards
Example: Laser Pointer, level/guide
Class 4:
May present a serious fire, skin or diffuse reflection hazard.
Output exceeds that of a Class 3
Recommend Class 1 installation (shielding, interlocks, etc.)
Example: CO2 and YAG laser tubes

Laser Safety References
• CDRH - Center for Devices and Radiological Health
– An agency within the U.S. Food and Drug Administration which publishes
and enforces legal requirements on laser product manufacturers. For copies
of the CDRH regulations, contact: CDRH (HFZ - 312), 2098 Gaither Road,
Rockville, MD 20850, Telephone (301) 594 - 4654
• ANSI/OSHA - ANSI (American National Standards Institute)
– U.S. organization that publishes standards for laser users. Their laser safety
standards are not laws, but they form the basis for state and OSHA
requirements for the use of lasers. For copies contact ANSI in New York at
(212) 642 - 4900 or the Laser Institute of America, at Telephone (407) 380 -
1553, Fax (407) 380 - 5588

– Does not have specific laser safety requirements. However, if they are
called into a facility, the inspectors follow published guidelines (OSHA
Instruction PUB 8 - 1.7 Guidelines for Laser Safety and Hazard Assessment)
that are based on an earlier (1986) ANSI Z136.1 standard.

Laser Sources
• JPT 20 Watt – 3 meter fiber length only
• JPT 30 Watt – 3 or 10 meter fiber length
• Pilot laser is generated by beam source
– Key switch must be turned in order to use
– Pilot laser is also used in Focus Finder function
– Shielding or safety glasses required when pilot active because cannot guarantee
no fiber beam is escaping/being generated since same source!!

Laser Sources
• Laser sources include fiber cable and collimator (all one part).
• Made by JPT
• There are no beam expansion modules in the collimator.
• Beam diameter at the output is 2.2 - 2.8 mm for CFT and CFS .
• The pulse width is at 200ns.
• Waveform at 200ns/25kHz
When setting laser type in Laser Configuration (Web Interface or TCS+/Service),
you must specify which length umbilical. If incorrect length errors will occur.
(Errors generated: E19512: Wrong System Config, E00107: Wrong Configuration, and E10507:
Error Reading Objective File)

Laser Sources – Power Measurement
• Use 30 kHz for both sources because difference in power per
pulse.
• 20W laser – 2/3 mJ per pulse x 30,000 pulses = 20 Watts expected output
• 30W laser – 1 mJ per pulse x 30,000 pulses = 30 Watts expected output
• Measure from following distance so that air knife does not cool power
meter target resulting in false/underpower reading.

Umbilical
• Umbilical is part of the laser source
• Cannot be changed separately
• Includes marking head cable, air hose for cooling head, and fiber
optic cable from source
• To replace umbilical you must change the beam source
• Min. bending radius of umbilical is 90 mm . The umbilical will be
made for stationary use only. No traversing applications!!

Cooling Compressor
Creates filtered, cooled air for marking unit and air knife across
protective glass
• Diaphragm pump with linear magnetic motor
• Lifetime of compressor: 16,000 - 20,000 hours expected
• Air into pump is pre - filtered by front fan filter mats
• 2 Filters – 1 in - line and 1 bulb style filter on pump
– Maintenance: Change filters on pump every 6 months
• Orientation matters!! Pump must be rotated 90 degrees if installing supply
unit in an orientation other than feet down.
• Air pressure monitored at LIC board – if pressure is too high or too low then
error will occur. Monitors pressure in millibars, not air flow (120 - 180 mb
acceptable)
• UADB (Universal Acceleration Detection Board) - detects the
orientation of the air pump. If the supply unit is installed vertically, the UADB
board generates an error message when the pump orientation was not changed
according to supply unit orientation.

Rescue Stick - Overview
▪ What is “Rescue Stick”? – USB written with an image file
containing manufacturer level software (enough to connect to Web
Interface). Use HDDRawCopyTool to write the image file so that the
USB is a bootable drive.
▪ Requirements: USB of at least 4 GB, CMark Rescue Image v. 1.7
or 1.11 (may also be named PlexMark ), HDDRawCopyTool
freeware. Files available on Sharepoint (Laser Software).
▪ When to use? – When multi - core Chameleon won’t boot.
▪ What does it do? – Erases CMark from flash and installs only
manufacturer level software ( PlexMark ). If corrupt system files were
the issue preventing booting this will recover board.
▪ What data is lost when using Rescue Stick? – All CMark files
including Customer Database (jobs, fonts, logos, parameters, etc.).
System Database (laser model, head type, IP addresses, etc.) are
retained.
▪ Try Rescue Stick at least twice before replacing board.

Stage 1
In a clean environment use e.g. an air bulb or ear syringe to blow contaminants off the opticalsurface. We do not recommend using compressed air or nitrogen lines because these may
contain their own contaminants (propellant, oil, etc.).
Stage 2
Place a lens tissue on the surface of the component. We recommend a soft lens tissue.
Place a drop of acetone on the tissue, wetting the enthe component. Note that a tissue moistened withtire surface, then slowly pull the tissue off solvent can absorb water vapor from the at-
mosphere, thus becoming useless after about 20 seconds. Discard the tissue after one use.
Stage 3
Fold a piece of lens paper one or more times to crevenly over the optical surface. Moeate a cushion so that pressure will be appliedisten the paper with the cleaning agent.
Holding the lens tissue with the index, middle and ring fingers, wipe across the component inone smooth, straight motion, covering the entire surface. We advise against holding the tissue
in forceps, because they will produce an uneven pressure. If the element is too large to be clea-ned in one swath, clean half with a C-shaped motion, then the other half in the opposite direc-
tion.
When cleaning mounted optics, you may need to fold the lens tissue into a triangle to reachinto the mounted edges.

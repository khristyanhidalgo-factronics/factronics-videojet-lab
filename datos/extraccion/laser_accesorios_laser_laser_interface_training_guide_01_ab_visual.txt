Safety
The laser system must only be used for the treatment of material surfaces. The surfaces are locally heated by intensive class 4
laser radiation and are modified thereby. These systems are mainly applied for marking of product surfaces (expiry dates, batch
printing, serial numbers, etc.).
The radiation produced by the laser system is of high energy and therefore improper use represents a danger to persons or
objects!
It is the policy of Videojet Technologies, INC to manufacture non- contact printing/coding systems and ink supplies which must
meet high performance and reliability quality standards. Videojet Technologies, INC enforces strict quality control techniques to
eliminate the potential for defects and hazards in its products.
The intended use of Videojet Technologies, INC Laser Marking Systems is to print information directly onto a product. Use of this
equipment in any other fashion may lead to serious personal injury.
The safety guidelines provided here are intended to educate all attending the presented course on all safety issues such that they
may install, operate, service, and maintain the subject printer (s) in a safe manner.

Danger of explosion! Make sure that the laser area is free of flam -

Arbitrary modifications or changes to the laser system are forbidden.
If a modification made by the user to a previously classified laser system leads to a change of its performance

Electrical Safety Guidelines
This section explains the safety guidelines related to electrical power supply, electrical cables, fuses, bonding, and grounding.
Electrical Power Supply
Personal Injury
Lethal voltages are present within this equipment when it is connected to the mains electrical supply. Only
trained and authorized personnel must carry out the maintenance work.
Personal Injury
Observe all statutory electrical safety codes and practices. Unless it is necessary to run the printer, disconnect
the printer from the mains electrical supply before removing the covers or attempting any service or repair
activity. Non- adherence to this warning can result in death or personal injury.
Personal Injury
A high AC voltage is present at the inverter and backlight. Extreme caution is required when diagnosing failure
in these areas.
Electrical Cables
Personal Injury
Use only the mains power cable supplied with the printer. This cable must terminate in an approved, three-
pole, mains plug which has a protective ground conductor.
l The electrical power cables, sockets, and plugs must be kept clean and dry.
l For pluggable equipment, the socket- outlet must be installed near the equipment and must be easily access -
ible.
Personal Injury
Always inspect the cables for damage, wear, corrosion, and deterioration. Make all grounding/bonding con -
nections void of areas of paint, ink build- up, and corrosion.

Bonding & Grounding
Personal Injury
The printer must be connected only to an AC power supply that has a protective ground conductor and must
be according to IEC requirements or applicable local regulations.
Personal Injury
Do not use the printer if there is any interruption in the protective ground conductor or if the protective ground
conductor is disconnected. The failure to follow this warning can cause an electrical shock.
Personal Injury
Always ground conductive equipment to an earthing electrode or to the building grounding system with
approved cables as per NEC standards in order to drain all potential static discharge. For example, a metal ser -
vice tray to earth ground.
Personal Injury
A resistance reading from the grounded service tray to the equipment chassis or mounting bracket should be 0
to less than 1 ohm. A resistance check should be made using a safe and reliable ohmmeter and should be
done on a frequent basis.
Personal Injury
The PCBs contain static sensitive devices. A suitably grounded, antistatic wrist strap must be worn when work -
ing on or handling PCBs.
Personal Injury
Always prevent static discharge from occurring. Use proper Grounding and Bonding methods. Only use Video -
jet approved metallic service trays and ground cables.
Personal Injury
Always bond conductive equipment together with approved cables to maintain them at the same potential
and minimize static discharge. For example, printhead to metal service tray.

Laser Interface Training Guide
Connect to the Web Interface
1. Before beginning work, ensure that you know the IP address of the laser system.
2. Set the PC's IP address so that it is on the same subnet as the laser. The first three octets of the IP address are the subnet.
For example: If the laser's IP address is 192.168.1.1 then you set the PC address to 192.168.1.x (x can be any number
from 2 - 254. So your IP address could be set to 192.168.1.5. If the subnet portion of the address (e.g. 192.168.1) is not
the same as the laser you will not establish a connection with the laser using any of the laser software interfaces.
If the laser's IP address is unknown, Dip Switch 1 can be moved to the ON position. After power cycling the laser the IP
address for Ethernet ports will be set to 192.168.1.1.
3. Connect a crossover Ethernet cable to the desired PC/laptop and to the Ethernet port on the rear of the supply unit. Altern -
atively, you can connect the laser side directly to one of the two Ethernet ports on the Chameleon board inside the supply
unit. This may be the case if a network cable were plugged into the standard Ethernet port and the optional user interface
port has a pinned connector.
4. Boot the laser (if not already booted). Do not attempt to connect until the laser has finished boot process and the white
light on the Supply Unit keypad has stopped flashing and is on solid.
5. Launch Internet Explorer program on PC. You may also use Firefox.
6. Type into the address field the IP address of the laser system and press Enter.
Specify Port to Connect to Multi– Core Chameleon Web UI
Multi- Core Chameleon boards host the TCS+ interface on the default HTTP port. You must add port
8080 to the address when entering it in your browser to access the Web UI instead of TCS+, e.g.
192.168.1.1:8080 .
The Web Interface Home screen should appear.

You can view or edit system database information through the Laser Web Interface. Should you replace the Chameleon board,
you must replace the default information in the board's system database with the correct settings for the laser in which you have

Disable No Trigger/Encoder Attached
Errors
When an external trigger source or encoder are specified in the Product Registration (Line Setup) the system looks for current
drain on specific outputs of the Customer Interface. The system monitors connector X25 pin 2 for Encoder and pin 4 for Trigger
(Product Detect) for a current draw of at least 20 mA. When the load is not present one of the following error messages is dis -
played: "E10813: No Encoder Connected" or "E10814: No Trigger Detector Connected." An example of this situation would be
using a dry contact for trigger or sharing one encoder for multiple lasers. Starting with CMark version 3.4 these errors may be dis -
abled through the laser's Web Interface.

File Tab Terms
The following terms are used within the File tab:
l Template
File that contains the layout of the marking, e.g. text, logos (graphics), dates, times, serial numbers, fonts, etc. In other
Videojet products would be a Template would be similar to a message or a job file.
l Logo
A graphical element that can be added to a template. Logos must be imported into the database before they can be used
in a Template.
l Database
A file which contains all templates, fonts, logos, parameter sets, and product registrations. Though multiple databases
may be created and saved on a computer, only one database at a time may be open on the computer. The laser system
contains a database
l Parameter Set
A type of file contains settings to adjust the laser system to the respective material of the product. For example, a para -
meter set contains settings for laser intensity and marking speed. which affect how deep the laser burns into the material.
Parameters sets can be adjusted through the Template tab.
l Product Registration
Type of file that contains all data necessary to detect a product and to trigger the marking process. Also includes inform -
ation on movement of the product and how that movement will be sensed.

Trigger Settings
The Triggers are subdivided into Start Trigger, Consecutive Trigger, and Stop Trigger. The availability of individual trigger settings
depends on the selected trigger type and the product movement.
l Start Trigger
Defines the settings for the first trigger pulse. The marking is started by the first trigger pulse.
With the trigger set to Auto , the marking process is started by pressing the Start button on control panel of the laser sys -
tem or in SmartGraph Laser Operation tab.
To start the marking process via an external signal you can define whether the Rising or the Falling edge of trigger pulse is
to be used as trigger.
l Consecutive Trigger
Defines the settings for all trigger pulses following the start trigger pulse.
If the consecutive trigger is set to None , only the start trigger pulse starts a marking process and no following pulses are
accepted. Select None as the Consecutive Trigger when you want to generate a single marking.
If the Consecutive Trigger is set to Auto , the marking process is triggered internally and continuously. The marking pro -
cess is repeated with a certain frequency or after the product has moved a certain distance, depending on the settings for
product movement.
To trigger the marking process via an external signal you can define whether the Rising or the Falling edge is to be used as
trigger.
l Stop Trigger
3 - 44
Rev AB

SmartGraph Software
Defines the settings for a trigger pulse that stops the marking process (e.g. the end of a cable or a stop signal from an
external control unit). The laser system remains ready to mark after a Stop Trigger pulse, which means that the marking
process will be continued if another Start Trigger pulse is detected.
If the Stop Trigger is set to None , then only pressing the Stop button on laser system or in SmartGraph | Laser Operation
will stop the marking process.
To trigger the marking process via an external signal you can define whether the Rising or the Falling edge is to be used as
trigger.
l Start Delay
Defines the distance until the marking process is started, which is the distance between the center of the marking field of
the laser system and the center of the marking on the product. After the trigger the product has to cover this distance
before it is in front of the marking head. If No Product Movement has been selected the Start Delay defines the time
between Start Trigger pulse and marking in milliseconds.
l Blocking
Defines the distance the product has to cover before the next marking can be started.
If No Product Movement has been selected the Blocking defines the time that has to pass after a Start Trigger until a Con -
secutive Trigger is accepted. This value should be 70 - 90% of the pitch (product length plus gap between products).
l Start Blocking
Sets the blocking time after the Stop Trigger pulse, where no external trigger pulses are accepted (debouncing) to start
marking.

the TU430 touchscreen via the TCS+ software. See "TU430 Screen Calibration" on page 7 .

on page 4 for activation instructions on lasers with an MMC/SD card. See "TCL Upload" on page 23 for activation instruc -
tions on lasers with a multi- core Chameleon board (which uses Flash instead of MMC/SD).
l TCP/IP is the communication interface default.
l All commands are available to customers in the Ultimate 6 Specification PDF .
l Videojet technicians may use Interface Monitor software tool to test functionality of Ultimate 6. This software is for
internal use only.

Set PC/Laptop IP Address
When using a computer to connect to the laser it is necessary to set the computer's IP address to one that will communicate with
the laser.
Known IP Address
Before beginning this task it is necessary to know the IP address assigned to the board (Ethernet port) that you
will be connecting to on the laser system. If IP of laser cannot be determined, then DIP Switch may be set to
ON and power on laser system cycled. The IP of laser will be set to 192.168.1.1. Any other address that was set
will still remain and when DIP Switch returned to OFF position and laser system power cycled then IP address
will be restored.
Changing PC IP Address
The following instructions assume use of Windows 7.
1. Click on Start in lower left hand corner of desktop.
2. Click Control Panel .
3. Select Network and Internet .
4. Click on Network and Sharing Center option.
5. Select Change Adapter Settings from the left hand column.
6. Double click on the Local Area Connection which is attached to the laser. If there is more than one LAN, connect Ethernet
cable to laser and power up laser. The Status for the LAN connected to the laser should change to Unidentified Network.

Appendices
7. Double click on Internet Protocol Version 4 (TCP/IPv4) option.
8. Click on the circle next to Use the Following IP Address .

9. Type in the address you would like to change your computer's Ethernet port to. For example, if the laser's IP is set to
192.168.1.1 then you would use the same first three octets, but must use a different last octet. In this case the last octet is 4.
If you use the same IP assigned to the laser then you will not be able to connect.
10. Click in the Subnet Mask box. The numbers 255.255.255.0 should fill the box. If it does not do this automatically then
type these numbers in as the Subnet Mask.

The primary system board of many Videojet lasers. Functionaly equivelant to a motherboard in a PC. The ACC3 is super -
ceded by the Chameleon board.
aperture
An opening, window, or lens through which optical radiation can pass. The aperture limits the energy or power for meas -
urement of exposure. (ANSI 136.1 - 2014)
B
beam divergence
Divergence is the increase in the diamater of the laser beam with distance from the beam waist, based on the full angle
at the point where the irradiance (or radiant exposure for pulsed lasers) is 1/e times the maximum value. (ANSI 136.1 -
2014)
beam expander
An optial device that expand the size of a collimated beam. There are generally two types, prismatic and telescopic.
Videojet beam expansion technology utlizes the latter telescopic, or Galilean, beam expansion method.
beam waist
The minimum diameter, in centimeters, of a focused, axis- symetric beam. For non- symetric beams, there may be a beam
waist along each major axis, each located at a different distance from the source. (ANSI 136.1 - 2014)
BTU (Beam Turning Unit)
Beam turning units are precisely machined, modular units containing mirrors inside internal elbow joints. BTUs may be
inserted between the laser head and marking head to redirect marking output to a location otherwise inaccesible to the
full laser head and marking head assembly.
BUS
Beam Unit Straight is a linear tube that allows the marking head to be located away from the laser head thus allowing
the marking head to fit into areas that would be inaccessible to full laser head and marking head assembly. Can be used

C
Controller
One of four main components of a laser system, the controller is the user interface device by which an operator directly
interacts with the laser system.
CPD
Cobra Power Distribution board is the primary safety controller in Videojet lasers that use Chameleon boards. The CPD
is the main interface for connections for interlock and shutterlock safety systems. The CPD is always present, but if a
higher performance level is necessary then a FASS board may be added to the CPD to bring to Performance Level D.
D
direct marking
From Smartgraph while editing a template, sending a template directly to a connected laser and firing the laser, marking
that template immediately.
DSP
Digital Signal Processor. Controls Laser source control, Marking trigger process, Marking head control, Marking process
E
ellipticity
A measurable ratio of the electromagnetic fields within a beam, relevant in the context of using devices to measure beam
size or power.
expansion factor
The value by which. through the application of a beam expander, the diamater of a laser beam diameter is increased.

Glossary
focal length
The distance from the secondary nodal point of a lens to the secondary focal point. For a thin lens imagina distant
source, the focal length is the distance between the lens and the focal point. (ANSI 136.1 - 2014)
G
gain medium
the source of optical gain in the laser, e.g. the contents of the beam source/laser tube.
I
ID Matrix
Another term for a Data Matrix code.
index step
Index steps allow you to use different marking parameters for various objects within the same template.
irradiance
Radiant power incident per unit area upon a surface, expressed in watts- per- centimeter- squared (W*cm^2). (ANSI 136.1
- 2014)
J
jump
A jump is movement of the scanner mirrors from the end of one stroke to the beginning of the next. The laser beam is
OFF during a jump.
jump delay
Jump Delay is a period of time prior to each jump that the galvos are not moved and the laser beam is OFF.
jump speed
Jump Speed is the speed at which the galvos move during a jump.

L
laser
A device that produces a nearly parallel, nearly monochromatic, and coherent beam of light by exciting atoms to a higher
energy level and causing them to radiate their energy in phase. Originally an acronym standing for (L)ightwave (A)m -
plification by (S)timulated (E)mission of (R)adiation.
laser head
One of four main components in a laser sytem, the laser head contains the laser tube and the Fast Move (FM) board.
laser off delay
At the end of each stroke, delay turning off the laser after the galvo mirrors stop moving.
laser on delay
At the start of each stroke, delay turning on the laser after starting to turn the galvo mirrors for marking movement.
Laser Tube
The hermetically sealed tube contaiining primary and secondary gases through which photons pass and are amplified
through stimulated radiation emission.
line width
The Line Width parameter allows you to tell the laser exactly how wide one line marks on the substrate in mm. Greatly
affected to the Overlap parameter.
LVDS
Low Voltage Differential Signal is the means of galvo signal commands from the ACC board to the FM Board or in lasers
with Chameleon boards to the CP- Light board.
M
mark delay
At the the end of each vector, delay the turning the galvo mirrors before marking the next vector.

Glossary
marking field
The two dimensional plane across which the laser system can mark. At its center is the origin of the X,Y axis (0,0). The X
and Y values on this axis correspond to the X and Y galvos and the positive/negative signal values they receive to mark
at a given location within the plane.
marking head
One of four main components in a laser system, the markin ghead contains the X and Y galvos that actively and dynam -
ically direct the beam at the substrate during marking.
marking intensity
Marking intensity is the strength of the beam. In Smartgraph you can adjust marking intensity as a percentage of the max -
imum power of the laser marking system.
marking speed
Marking speed is the speed at which galvos move when the laser beam is on.
Matrix
A matrix splits a single template into multiple discrete cells, each of which can contain unique image and text content or
a repeated pattern of the same image and text content.
MMC
An MMC or Multimedia Card is a flash memory storage device on which the laser operating system is stored. MMCs are
a common flash storage product but have been superseded by SD Cards in both general use and in Videojet lasers.
mode quality
Mode Quality (or M^2) is a measurement of beam quality expressed a ration between the measured beam and a perfect
diffraction- limited Gaussian beam which would have an M^2 = 1.
O
off delay
At the end of each stroke, delay turning off the laser after the galvo mirrors stop moving.
on delay
At the start of each stroke, delay turning on the laser after starting to turn the galvo mirrors for marking movement.

overlap
The Overlap parameter determines how closely to the last line each new line is marked. Overlap is set as a percentage
of total line width from - 50% to 50% with 0% representing no overlap at all.
P
Pilot Laser
A small, visiible laser that demonstrates where the marking head will mark. The pilot laser does not mark on substrate.
polarization
In most cases, laser light is linearly polarized. This means that the electric field oscillates in a particular spatial direction.
pulse frequency
Lasers mark not with a continuous beam but rather with rapid laser pulses (CO2, YAG, Fibers). The pulses are marked
close together and overlap so that each stroke appears to be a solid line. The Pulse Frequency parameter allows your
control over how frequent the pulses are, i.e. how much they will overlap.
R
rise time
The time required for a pulse to rise from 10 percent to 90 percent of its steady value.
S
SD Card
Secure Digital (SD) is a non- volatile memory card format developed by the SD Card Association (SDA) for use in port -
able devices. The standard was introduced in August 1999 by joint efforts between SanDisk, Panasonic (Matsushita
Electric) and Toshiba as an improvement over MultiMediaCards (MMC), [1] and has become the industry standard. SD
Cards supersede MMCs on ACCs revision L or later.
SLEB (Steered Laser Extension Board)
Adds dedicated Ethernet port, Serial connection (RS- 232), and External Job Selection functionality.
spot size

by its frequency, and to the speed of a wave times its period.

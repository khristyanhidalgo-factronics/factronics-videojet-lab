The relevant European directive is 91/689/EC Hazardous W aste . 91/689/EC Hazardous W aste
encompasses the European W aste Catalogue under which new clean filters would be classified under
item 15 02 03, “absorbents, filter materials, wiping clothes and protective clothing other than those
mentioned in 15 02 02”. This is a non hazardous category .
However , depending on the materials being lasered, the fume which is collected in the filters may be
considered dangerous in which case the classification changes to 15 02 02 M. This is a potentially
Hazardous category depending on the amount of hazardous material in the filter and the risk it poses.
For the pre-filter , the risk involved would normally be R37, “Irritant to respiratory system,” caused by
the very fine particulate trapped in the filter . However , this only becomes hazardous if more than 20%
of the waste (filter plus particulate) is made up of the hazardous material, which is unlikely PVC .
applications have an added dimension in that HCl is generated and in some circumstances this
could potentially form an acid inside the filter . This would be dif ficult to quantify , so we
recommend that the filter in such an application is treated as a hazardous waste with a potential R34
Corrosive risk.
The combined filter needs to be considered in the same way with the risks being dif ferent for dif ferent
materials. Most applications will be non hazardous because the percentage of any hazardous material
is likely to be below the threshold level.
Again, for PVC and other polymers which generate highly toxic gasses, it is safest to treat the filters as
hazardous waste with a toxic risk.

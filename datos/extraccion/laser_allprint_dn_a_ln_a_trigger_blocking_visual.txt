Notes for Using the Trigger Blocking The trigger blocking is used to debounce trigger signals. The following figure shows five exem-
plary configurations with trigger pulses given by products all 100ms. Each signal has a lengthof approx. 12ms and the laser has been configured to react to the positive edge of the signal.
The green area is the time in which the system rtrigger input is blocked, noeacts to trigger pulses. In the red areas the trigger pulses are accepted.
Case AThis is the ideal case. The trigger signal has clear edges, no bounces and each signal has the
same length. In this case the blocking time is irrelevant since there are no additional triggeredges except the actual trigger edges of the signal. However, in reality this ideal case is ex-
tremely rare.
Case BIn case B the signal bounces on the rising edge. Instead of one edge there are three for each
product. Without blocking this would result in three markings per product. The blocking of90ms safely prevents those multiple triggers.
Case CHere the signal bounces on the falling edge. The same applies as to case B: the blocking safely
prevents multiple triggers.

Case EThe last example illustrates that a highly disturbed signal cannot be compensated even by the
trigger blocking. Just after the blocking time a new trigger is caused by the disturbance. Thusthe second product is marked far too early. Since the blocking starts immediately afterwards,
and therefore also too early, a disturbance is interpreted as trigger also for the third product.Thus also the third product is marked too early.
In this case you can only make sure that the input signal has no disturbances.

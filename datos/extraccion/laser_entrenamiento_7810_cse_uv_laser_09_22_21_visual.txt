Depth of Field
Definition:
The depth of field is the area, where the diameter of the laser beam is smaller the
twice the focal diameter.
Knowledge:
The bigger the beam expansion factor is, the smaller the laser beam can be focused.
The bigger the beam expansion factor is, the smaller is the depth of field.
The bigger the focal length is, the bigger is the depth of field.
Confidential - Company Proprietary

Wavelength Vs. the Eye
Laser radiation in the invisible, short wave UV - A range (wavelength 315
nm - 380 nm) of low intensity can cause inflammation of the cornea
( photokeratitis ) or of the conjunctiva ( photoconjunctivitis ) as well as
cataract (clouding of the eye lens). In case of higher radiation intensity it
can cause reversible corneal opacity, irreversible corneal opacity and
cataracts.

Photokeratitis
http://informationng.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/photokeratitis.jpg
Photokeratitis or ultraviolet keratitis is a painful eye condition caused by exposure of
insufficiently protected eyes to the UV rays from either natural or artificial sources.
Photokeratitis is akin to a sunburn of the cornea and conjuntiva , and is not usually noticed until
several hours after exposure. Symptoms include increased tears and a feeling of pain, likened to
having sand in the eyes.

General safety
Class 1:
Not capable of emitting accessible laser radiation in access of what
the AEL allows for class one.
Example: Laser Printer, CD player
Class 2:
Not capable of emitting accessible laser radiation greater than AEL
allows with .25 exposure duration. Less than 1mw.
Example: Pilot laser and He - Ne alignment Laser
Class 3:
Potentially hazardous if focused at the eye or skin for less time than
what’s allowed for class 2. Less than 500 mw.
May cause acute eye hazards
Example: Laser Pointer, level/guide
Class 4:
May present a serious fire, skin or diffuse reflection hazard. Output
exceeds that of a Class 3. Greater than 500mw.
Recommend Class 1 installation (shielding, interlocks, etc.)
Example: CO2 and YAG laser tubes

UV Laser PPE Controls
1. Engineering controls (enclosure and Barriers)
• Enclose UV laser beam paths to extent practical
• Design barriers or enclosures for beam dumps to minimize potential diffuse
exposures.
2. Administrative Procedures
• Attenuate laser beam and plan work to minimize exposure time
• Keep exposed skin as far as practical from open beams
3. Skin PPE
• Wear long sleeved shirts
• Use gloves when working with hands accessible laser beams (primary or stray)
• Use gloves when diffuse reflection NHZ> 20cm, if working within that distance
• Use face shield when diffuse reflection NHZ> 1m if working within that distance
4. Medical exams
• Periodic skin exams recommended for laser personnel who may have exposures >
MPE
5. Site specific training
• OJT and SOP must describe potential for skin injury and controls to use

UV - Resonator – Schematic Diagram
UV light

pump laser SHG THG

The laser (Vanadate) is pumped by the pump diode and generates infrared light (IR,
1064nm). Then the IR light is doubled in the SHG crystal (Green. 532nm) and tripled in the
THG crystal (UV, 355nm). Only the UV light is coupled out of the optical setup. Pulses are
generated in the setup with the Q - switch which blocks the beam first and then allows the
light to resonate between the end mirrors so that a pulse can be generated.

Marking Intensity
The laser parameter marking intensity significantly affects the quality of the marking.
It alters the power of the laser marking system.
The marking intensity is adjustable from 0 to 100%.
Under 70% leads to an unstable marking.
60KHz @ 100% Intensity = 1 watt measure 24 amps DC
20KHz @ 100% Intensity = 2 watts measure 60 amps DC
Note: With UV laser, changes to Intensity lead to an approximately 70 sec delay as crystal
stabilizes. Avoid Intensity changes in Indexes ,if possible, by adjusting frequency or Q - switch
width. Those do not result in a delay.

CIP Value (Current in Percent)
• CIP is the actual current value in percent.
- Value of 0 corresponds to a pump current of 0% and a value of 1 equals 100%.
- The current value of the nominal power and frequency is specified on the data
sheet that accompanies the beam source, e.g. Current(CIP) = 46.74%
Depending on the system, the CIP roughly ranges from 40% to mid - 70%.
• For the CIP there is a rollover point at which the system can no longer consume
power. From that point on the power decreases with increasing current.
• Power Correction value must be entered in Laser Configuration as a decimal value.
If CIP value on datasheet is 39.74%, then divide by 100 (equals .3974) and round
up to .40.
• CIP value comes from Coherent and is necessary to reference the beam source at 1
Watt (max output power) at 100% Intensity, 60kHz Frequency and 2 microsecond Q -
switch Width.
• Power Correction value can be changed after thousands of working hours in order
to compensate for the power loss due to the aging of the laser.

Some Notes
• Expected lifetime of the laser beam source: 15,000 - 25,000 hours of beam on time (2 year warranty)
• Power measurement settings :
Marking Intensity : 100%
Frequency : 20kHz
Q - Switch Width: 2µs
Underneath the lens: approx. 2W (in cw - mode below 1mW)
• 26A (2,2Volt) measured with amp clamps with power measurement settings.
• Marking Intensity less than approx . 75%, leads to an unstable marking.
• Laser On Delay: 7 minutes (after closing the key - switch.)
System state : ‘Start Laser‘.
• Default Power Change Delay: 20 seconds or more (can be 70 seconds; if you have change in Intensity setting
then there will be a delay — including between Indexes if Intensity different between Indexes)
System state : ‘ Prepare for Marking ‘.
• After the first marking wait for 2 hours until the beam source runs stable.
Only neccessary for very high quality requirements and after the system was turned off for several hours.
It‘s neccessary to warm up the SHG/THG crystals to approx. 40 ° C, in order to achieve optimal conversion and a
good output power stability.

Error Codes from COS
1. ‘ E00200: Laser not ready ‘: If power supply error or communication error between controller
and beam source occurs .
2. ‘ E00202: Laser overtemperature ‘: If error code ‘ Temperature too high‘ or ‘ Temperature heat
sink laserhead too high‘ or ‘ Temperature laserdiodes too high‘
is received from the Matrix source .
3. ‘ E00204: General error ‘: If error code ‘Laserdiode(s)down‘ or one of the other errors is received from
the Matrix source .
4. ‘ E00212: Wrong LIC detected ‘: If a wrong LIC is inserted in LIC interface .
‘ Laser not ready ‘ is the only error code which indicates a possible failure of the
laser power supply .
‘ Laser overtemperature ‘ and ‘ General error ‘ are generated, when the beam source fails.
They are related to the next error codes pages .
Means the Matrix UV - beam source has to be exchanged .

“ Additional Information: 0x1001 Date, Time, W19512 “

When this warning is generated, the beam source is defective.
The Coherent code matches to the Error code lists on the next pages.
Parallel, the Error “ E00202 Laser overtemperature “ or “ E00204 General error “ is generated.

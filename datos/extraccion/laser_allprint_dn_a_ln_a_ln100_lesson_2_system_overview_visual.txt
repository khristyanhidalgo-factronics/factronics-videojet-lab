1.1 Structure of a lamp pumped Nd: YAG laser
A laser pumped with an arc lamp or a flash lamp is usually pumped through the lateral wall of
the lasing medium, which is often in the form of a crystal rod containing a metallic impurity or a
glass tube containing a liquid dye, in a condition known as "side - pumping." To use the lamp's
energy most efficiently, the lamps and lasing medium are contained in a reflective cavity that
will redirect most of the lamp's energy into the rod. Nd: YAG is a Neodymium - Doped Yttrium -
Aluminum - Garnet (lasing material used in near - infrared lasers)

For generation of the laser light a Nd:YAG crystal is used. This is an Y ttrium A luminium G arnet

lasers are classified into lamp - pumped and diode - pumped.

1.1.4 The krypton lamp module
Flash lamp and arc lamps are high - intensity discharge devices commonly used in photography
and laser technology and usually contain gases such as xenon and krypton. The flash or arc is
initiated by a high voltage across the discharge tube, which in turn ionizes the gas and produces
a high - intensity light with output peaks in both the visible and infrared regions of the
electromagnetic spectrum.
Discharge lamps used for laser pumping are grouped in two categories: arc lamps and flash
lamps. Arc lamps are optimized for continuous - wave operation , whereas flash lamps (flash
lamps) produce pump pulses for either free - running or Q - switched lasers . (Note that the term
flash lamp is often wrongly used instead of arc lamp .) Both types of lamps essentially consist of
a glass tube, filled with some gas (e.g. krypton at a few atmospheres) and having a metallic
electrode at each end.

1.3.1 Structure and function of a Q - Switch
Acousto - optic modulators (AOM) can be used to alter the amplitude, direction, frequency, and
polarization of a laser beam. Principle: a piezoelectric transducer is mounted onto a transparent
solid (e.g. a tellurium oxide (TeO2), dense flint glass, fused quartz block). Via an rf signal
the transducer is set vibrating. The resulting acoustic (pressure) wave propagates through the
optical medium causing a periodic density and therefore a periodic refraction index change in the
medium. The material behaves like a sinusoidal grating. An incident light beam passing this
grating is diffracted into several orders. With appropriate design and alignment of the AOM the
first order beam has the hig h est intensity. Its angular deviation and intensity can be adjusted via
the rf frequency and power
The deflection ang le θB ≈ λ frf / va
The efficiency η ≈ sin2 (const Urf / λ)
A resonator - internal AOM can Q - switch the laser by deflecting the laser beam out of the
optical axis and back (laser light off/ laser light on). A resonator - external AOM can be used
to angular de flect the laser beam .
The RF - driver delivers a constant frequency of 27,1 MHz to the transducer.
By power change, a more or less strong acoustic wave is produced.
This wave breaks the beam. The absorber neutralizes the acoustic wave, so that it could not be
reflected.

In most of the cutting edge laser machinery, laser optics will be incorporated into system to
realize various applications like marking, drilling, welding, dicing, scribing, cutting, engraving
etc. F - theta scanning lens is used in conjunction with X and Y galvanometer scanner and beam
expander to form a two dimensions scanning system. The scan lens is optimized to provide a flat
field image with diffraction - limited spot size in micron scale over the entire scan field. The
image field height is almost directly proportional to the scan angle. Typically, the linearity f -
theta distortion is kept less than 1% without software correction to ensure minimal distortion.
High Trans missive AR coating is coated onto the lens elements surface to ensure minimal

Consequences:
Due to its shorter wavelength t he Nd:YAG laser can generate (at least theoretically) ten times
thinner lines and 100 times higher intensities at a product surface than a comparable CO2 laser:
Focal diameter dF (Nd:YAG) = 1/10 * dF (CO2)
Intensity I (Nd:YAG) = 100 * I (CO2)
Therefore whenever high resolution and/ or high intensity is required the YAG laser is
the first choice provided the material effects are suited.
A laser operated in the TEM00 mode generates the best beam quality possible. Its B factor
equals to 1. With decreasing laser beam quality the B factor increases strongly and consequently
the spot size while the intensity decreases.

1. 10.4.8 Intensity Distribution of different laser modes
The standing light waves that principally can build up in the resonator are called laser
modes. The ’active’ laser modes make up the laser beam and therefore determine its
light distribution. Which modes principally can occur and which of these modes really become
active is determined by the resonator design and the laser operating parameters (pump power
etc.). Dependent on this only one mode can make up the laser beam or several modes
simultaneously. Accordingly we distinguish between single - mode and multi - mode operation of
a laser. Figure Fig. 4 indicates possible modes of a laser. These so - called T ransverse
E lectromagnetic M odes are distinguished by the indices mn. The TEM00 mode (also denoted
fundamental or Gaussian mode) comes with a
Gaussian energy distribution across the beam and the best laser beam quality which is possible
for a laser. As higher the order of the mode (TEM00 TEM01* TEM11 ...) as lower the laser
beam quality.

2. 5 System Overview
The Advanced Controller Card (ACC) is the main circuit board of the laser system. The ACC
communicates via CAN bus and optical fibre with other boards of the system controlling and
monitoring components. The ACC contains two processors:
The PowerPC controls the communication with the PC ( Smart Graph software) and
administrates the application database on the MultiMediaCard (MMC).
The Digital Signal Processor (DSP) receives prepared marking data from the PowerPC and
transmits the commands for positioning the galvo mirrors to the FM and for generating the laser
pulses to the LPC. Thus the DSP is responsible for the actual marking.
The Laser Power Controller (LPC) and the Laser Head Controller (LHC) are controlling and
monitoring the laser power supply, the cooling system and the laser beam source.
The Fast Move (FM) board in the marking head of the laser system controls the galvo mirrors.
The Customer Interface (CI) comprises all interfaces: network (ethernet), serial interface,
digital inputs and outputs for system states and commands, digital inputs for connecting trigger
signals and encoders.

Local errors
Two LEDs, the “K - bus LEDs”, indicate the operational state of the bus terminals and the connection to
these bus terminals. The green LED lights up in order to indicate fault - free operation. The red LED blinks
with two different frequencies in order to indicate an error. The error is encoded in the blinks as follows:
1. fast blinking: Start of the error code
2. first slow sequence: Error code
3. second slow sequence: Error code argument
Errors:
Passive bus terminals, such as a power feed terminal, are not included in the terminal count.
In the case of some errors, rectification does not cause the bus coupler to leave the blink sequence. The
bus coupler can only be re - started either by switching the power supply off and on again, or by a software
reset.

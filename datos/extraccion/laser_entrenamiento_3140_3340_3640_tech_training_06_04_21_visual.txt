Lens Hazard
The laser system contains optics constructed from Zinc Selenide. When broken into
small pieces or exposed to acidic conditions, they may give rise to harmful products.
In normal use however, the laser optical components are not considered to be
hazardous.
In the event that a laser optical component is damaged in any way, the material and any
packaging must be treated as hazardous waste and disposed of accordingly.

Laser Accidents
• Statistically laser accidents are due to several
factors:
• Eye exposure unexpectedly during alignment,
eye protection not being worn, equipment
malfunction, high voltage mishandling, non -
beam hazards, incorrect eyewear, inhalation of
air contaminants, viewing of laser - generated
plasmas, and in general using equipment
incorrectly
• The most common type of injury is to the eye
(around 70%). There have been a few deaths,
mostly caused by electrocution or fires.

General Safety
Class 1:
Not capable of emitting accessible laser radiation in excess of what
the AEL allows for class one.
Example: Laser Printer, CD player
Class 2:
Not capable of emitting accessible laser radiation greater than AEL allows
with .25 exposure duration.
▪ Example: Pilot laser and He - Ne alignment Laser
Output must be less than 1mW
Class 3:
Potentially hazardous if focused at the eye or skin for less time than what’s
allowed for class 2
May cause acute eye hazards
▪ Example: Laser Pointer, level/guide
Output is greater than 1mW but less than 0.5W
Class 4:
May present a serious fire, skin or diffuse reflection hazard. Output
exceeds that of a Class 3.
Output is greater than 0.5W
▪ Example: CO2 and YAG laser tubes
Recommend Class 1 installation (shielding, interlocks, etc )

– An agency within the U.S. Food and Drug Administration which publishes

• ANSI/OSHA - ANSI (American National Standards Institute)
– U.S. organization that publishes standards for laser users. Their laser safety
standards are not laws, but they form the basis for state and OSHA
requirements for the use of lasers. For copies contact ANSI in New York at
(212) 642 - 4900 or the Laser Institute of America, at Telephone (407) 380 -
1553, Fax (407) 380 - 5588
• OSHA - The Occupational Safety & Health Administration
– Does not have specific laser safety requirements. However, if they are called
into a facility, the inspectors follow published guidelines (OSHA Instruction
PUB 8 - 1.7 Guidelines for Laser Safety and Hazard Assessment) that are
based on an earlier (1986) ANSI Z136.1 standard.

Depth of Focus means that we can stay in focus even on surfaces not perfectly flat. But we must

threshold of interaction with the material preventing proper and well - contrasted markings.

with focal 100th The laser spot is 3 times as large, but the depth of field increases by 6 times.

Leakage Protection
The Good: This feature closes Shutter after 9 seconds of no trigger.
Example would be customer’s production line slows or stops, but laser is still in
waiting for trigger state.
The Bad: This feature can cause issues when shutter ( galvos ) cannot
open and get to start position before product exits mark window.
Faults can range from Product Movement Too Fast, Beam Shutter errors, or
even X - or Y - Mirror errors.
Whenever errors are
appearing after conveyor
slow or stop while laser still
in marking mode (waiting
for trigger) turn off Leakage
Protection in Web Interface
and reboot.
Always enabled by default.

TCS versus TCS+
TCS
• Installed on a PC (version 5.x is for Chameleon; version 2.x is only for Ice board
(3020/3030).
• Used with Single Core Chameleon and early multi - core Chameleon. Support for
TCS (pc - based) ended with CMark 4.5.
• TCS version needs to be matched to CMark (list of matches on Sharepoint )
• TU420 with 8 - pin connector available (Windows touchscreen PC inside stainless
steel box; one TU420 per laser needed if using).
• Limited features including need for SmartGraph for file conversion and loading if
additional fonts or logos are needed.
TCS+
• Runs natively from laser. Always part of CMark since version 4.x.
• Since it is part of CMark, TCS+ never needs to be updated separately.
• Access TCS+ via web browser (IP address) or with TU430/TU440. TU430/TU440
can only be used with TCS+. It is a touchscreen monitor not a PC.
• Faster because not relying on PC processor.
• Features available have continued to increase and as of CMark 4.9 has many of the
features of both SmartGraph and Web Interface.

Red Emissions LED Defective
Symptom: E00210 Red Emissions LED Defective received when
keyswitch is armed.
Red LED on marking head and on keypad are part of the safety circuit and
laser power will not be enabled if either one of the LEDs is bad.
• With power to laser system off, disconnect umbilical connector at rear of laser
head and inspect pins on male side. Look specifically for bent pins or pins which
are not sticking out as far as the others. Specifically focus on pins 31 and 32.
• Check forward and backward resistance on the membrane keypad pins 2 - 10. If
open, replace the keypad.
• Check the resistance between leads on the marking head LED. If open, replace
the LED.
• Check continuity on shutter cable for HDLED_EMIS_RET and
MBLED_EMIS_RET wires.
- If open from HIC to Umbilical Connector replace umbilical.
- If open from Umbilical Connector to CP - Light, replace laser head harness.

General Information – Protocol/ Datastring
• Encoding UTF - 8
• Parameter Delimiter is Semicolon (0x3B)
• End Delimiter is Carriage Return [CR] and Line Feed [LF] (0x0D, 0x0A)
• Returns Acknowledged [ACK] or NotAcknowledged [NAK] (0x06, 0x15)
• Command
Commandname;Arguments ;<CR><LF>
• Answer
<ACK>; ResultCode;Arguments ;<CR><LF>
<NAK>; ResultCode ;<CR><LF>
• Event
Event;EventName;Arguments ;<CR><LF>

Troubleshooting Ultimate 6
1) Are activated files installed on SD/MMC card or uploaded via Web
Interface?
a) Don’t drag and drop to copy/paste to SD card.
b) Were files copied/pasted in Smarties/customer/ tcl ? Don’t paste to
smarties/customer.
c) Use 7 - zip to zip files for upload via Web Interface/TCS+ not Windows
zip.
2) Is interface type correct? Set by default to TCP/IP. If RS - 232 then
Setup.txt file must be edited.
3) Are comms settings correct? If TCP/IP is customer port number set to
20001? If RS - 232, are baud rate, data bits, stop bits, etc. set correctly?
4) If an existing customer switching from previous SAR for comms (such
as SAR 09258), ensure they are using Ultimate commands and not
commands from previous SAR. Use Interface Monitor tool to
send/receive and compare to what customer sent/received. Commands
are different than previous SARs.

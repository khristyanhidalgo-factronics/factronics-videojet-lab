1.4 Marking Elements
The following elements must be defined in order to mark a product:
Marking ContentsThe marking content defines the layout of the marking, e.g. textelements, logos, serial numbers, fonts, etc.
Parameter SetParameter sets adjust the laser syof the product. A parameter set contains e.g. information on thestem to the respective material
laser power or the marking speed.
ProductRegistationThe product registration contains all data necessary to detect theproduct and to trigger the marking process.
Positioning DataThe positioning data defines the positioning and the size of themarking on the product.
The combination of the four elements marking content, parameter set, product registration andpositioning within a template offers the possibility to adapt the marking to different marking
tasks in a very short period of time.
Examples:
You want to mark a certain text on synthetic material instead of paper. Hence you change your parameter set from »paper« to »synthetic material«.
You want to change the position of the marking on you product. For that you just change the positioning data directly in your template or in the user interface »Laser Operation« under
»Marking settings«.

Templates can be joined to sequences or batches. The templates of a sequence or batch areexecuted one after the other. The following distinction is made:
SequenceA trigger pulse starts a succession of templates which are marked on theproduct one after the other. Trigger pulses coming in while executing a
sequence are put into a trigger queue and executed after finishing thesequence.
BatchThe templates of a batch are executed by consecutive trigger pulses. Thefirst pulse comes from the start trigger, all following pulses are consecutive
trigger pulses (product registration). If the execution is interrupted, it has tobe started again with the start trigger, i.e. the execution begins with the first
template in the batch.
At the end of a batch or sequence the execution is started again with the first template.

incremental encoder.
»Distance/Rot.« defines the distance the product covers with one full turn of the incrementalencoder.
»Pulses/Rot.« states the number of pulses the incremental encoder emits when performingone full turn.
ImportantThe number of pulses per rotation depends on the type of incrementalencoder used and on whether one or two tracks are utilized.
In case that two tracks are used, the number of pulses per rotation given forthe incremental encoder has to be doubled and entered at »Pulses/Rot.«.
»Rotational direction monitoring« defines the direction of rotation of the incremental encoder.It is used e.g. to cut out signals that derive from slipping of the conveyor belt. You can select
clockwise or counterclockwise rotation. Select »None« to turn the detection of the direction ofrotation off.
